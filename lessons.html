What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when fit. White was the canonical case: ~1.0/GW projected while scoring 11, 7, 2 on three 90s; a White sale topped the board for five straight gameweeks and was correctly declined every time — until GW5, when FPL flagged him d 75% and the sale went through on minutes, not on xP. The artefact is never the reason to sell; an availability flag is. Applied again in GW6 (Semenyo, d 75%, one week after he scored 17) and in GW7 (Szoboszlai, d 75% hamstring, 0 mins in GW6). Ødegaard is the fading hold case — 2.6/GW projected against 11, 3, 10, 3, 2, 1 actual. The GW6 blank on 89 minutes is the first real evidence the artefact has worn off; if he blanks in GW7 he becomes the sale.
  • GK override, resolved. The model was badly wrong on Kinsky early (0.9 → 6; 1.1 → 7). The GW6 reversal to Lammens cost exactly 1 point (Lammens 2, Kinsky 3). Verdict: a wash. Start whichever has the better fixture and stop re-litigating it weekly.
  • Defensive-contribution players are systematically under-projected, but that floor is conditional on minutes. M.Sangaré: 14, 4, 0 (45 mins), 4, 0 (21 mins), 1 (38 mins) against 0.7/GW. Now dead weight whose upgrade does not change the XI; sale earmarked after Maguire.
  • none quality means unknown, not bad.
  • Scenario Gain overstates real gain twice over — once when the outgoing player is benched, again when the incoming player only starts by displacing an artefact.
  • The scenario table only validates transfers one at a time. It does not check that a pair of rows is jointly affordable. Re-run the combined arithmetic from sell prices and give the funding order.
  • The board-topping row is often not the right row. GW7: Szoboszlai → Dewsbury-Hall (+7.3) lost to Szoboszlai → Groß (+5.3) on set-piece duty, elite ownership cover, £0.6m of headroom that funds the next move, and fixture correlation (DH would have made it four players in EVE v CHE). Say the xP cost of the override out loud and compare it to the model's own MAE (2-3/GW) before taking it.
  • The best use of a marginal FT is a hole, not an upgrade.
  • The model's own best XI ignores injury flags and will start a 75%-doubt player — it started Szoboszlai in GW7. Always re-check against the saved XI and the availability block.
  • The model has no view on the vice-captain. Check it against the flags every week. GW7 was the first week it was already correct (Mbeumo) without intervention.
  • Vice must not share the captain's fixture. The eligible vice rotates between Mbeumo and whichever mid is outside Haaland's match.
  • Advice execution: GW1-GW6 mostly ignored; GW7 found GW6's advice executed in full — both transfers, the lineup switch and the vice. Keep leading with the free changes, but the assumption that nothing gets done is no longer safe either. Always re-read slots 1-11 and state the delta.
  • Verify which club a sale actually frees, and which player actually left. Count clubs from the squad table, never from the previous narrative.
  • Club-cap discipline beats a 3-point xP edge. Guéhi is listed at MCI here; buying him caps MCI alongside Haaland. Declined GW3-GW7.
  • Fixture correlation is invisible to the model. Name it every week, including correlations a transfer creates.
  • Dataset club labels drift from reality (Guéhi and Semenyo listed MCI, Rogers listed CHE). Follow the table for cap arithmetic, and say so when it looks odd.
  • Price/net-transfer columns and LiveFPL elite-vs-xEO gaps are the only crowd signal when search fails. A large elite-above-crowd gap (Groß, Palmer, Calafiori in GW7) shows where informed money already is. Use it for direction, never quote it as fact, and never let it be the sole reason for a move.
  • Model bias: GW1 -2.38 (MAE 4.88), GW2 -2.83 (3.18), GW3 -1.06 (2.52), GW4 -0.10 (1.98), GW5 -1.46 (3.35), GW6 -0.33 (2.38). Pessimistic on average; misses are under-predictions of nailed in-form players. Distrust a low number on a player who just played 90.
  • Chip discipline. 3xc was pencilled for GW7 (Haaland 8.2 v IPS H) and then declined: +8 is the floor of what a chip should earn, and the same 8.2 recurs at GW11 (FUL H). Rule adopted — take 3xc by ~GW15 on the best remaining Haaland home fixture if no double or 9+ projection appears. First-half chips expire GW19. Wildcard: HOLD, re-test GW9. BB needs a 12+ bench; it has projected 4-10 all season.
  • Web search unreliable in this harness (GW1-GW7, all empty). Say so plainly; make calls conditional on the manager checking the presser; never substitute recall.
