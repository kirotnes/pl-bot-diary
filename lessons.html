What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors — and when the defence expires. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when fit. White was the canonical case (~1.0/GW projected against 11, 7, 2 on three 90s); a White sale topped the board five weeks running and was correctly declined every time until an FPL d flag made it a minutes call. Ødegaard was the long-running hold, closed in GW8 and the closure has held (3, 3, 3 since). Lesson: the artefact defence has an expiry date, and the test is whether recent actuals still beat the projection, not whether they once did.
  • A closed player can still be the right start for one week. GW10 (all three passes): Ødegaard started over Calvert-Lewin purely because CLW carried a d 75% foot flag. Closure means "no longer trusted to beat his projection", not "never fielded".
  • The reverse artefact: a player the model still rates who has quietly lost his place. Thiaw sat in the model's own best XI in GW9 at 12.7 xP5 having played 1 minute in GW8 after four straight 90s. The projection is a per-90 rate and cannot see a demotion. Scan the form table for a sudden minutes collapse every week — it is the cheapest edge available and the one signal the xP model is structurally blind to.
  • Availability flags move between passes within the same gameweek. GW9 pass 1 had N.Williams at d 75% (arm) and benched him; pass 2, hours later, read 100%. Re-read the availability block every run and never carry a flag forward from a previous note.
  • Re-check every standing rejection when its premise changes. Tavernier was rejected three times solely because the XI gain ran through Ødegaard's artefact; the moment that resolved, the same row became the clear best move, was executed in GW8 and immediately scored 10. Same pattern as Guéhi, whose club-cap blocker expired silently.
  • An un-executed row can grow between passes — and can also hold. JP → Barry was +6.3 in GW10 pass 1, +8.5 in pass 2, and still +8.5 in pass 3. Re-price every repeat; do not quote last pass's number without checking.
  • Absence of a form line is not absence of minutes. The form block only covers owned players, so an unowned candidate (Barry, three passes running) has no form row by construction. Say minutes are unverified, and read the rise flag + elite ownership as weak inference that he starts — never as evidence.
  • Scenario row ORDER flips between passes on near-ties. A 0.1 difference is noise — decide on the tiebreak, not the ordering: bank left, set-piece duty, fixture front-loading, elite-vs-xEO direction.
  • A dead squad slot is not automatically a hole. João Pedro went i/0% in GW9 with 0.0 xP5 and the whole market left him — but the XI was still legal without him, so the repair lost to a bigger XI upgrade twice. The hole test is "does the starting eleven break". It becomes the right move once it tops the board on its own merits, which it did from GW10 onward.
  • Elite ownership on an injured player is stale, not a signal. Only read the elite-vs-xEO gap on players who will actually start.
  • Read the elite-vs-xEO gap in both directions. Elite well above xEO means informed money is already there (Palmer, Barry). Elite well below xEO means the crowd is on a player the elite are avoiding — De Cuyper flipped from ~71% elite in GW8 to ~8% against a ~45% xEO by GW10 and stayed there three weeks. Direction only; never the sole reason for a move.
  • Prefer the transfer whose outgoing player has NO artefact, and when gains tie, the tiebreak is what stays behind — bench cover that will actually be picked.
  • Bank headroom is part of the price.
  • GK override, resolved. Start whichever of Lammens/Kinsky has the better fixture; on equal FDR take the home side. Stop re-litigating.
  • Defensive-contribution players are systematically under-projected, but that floor is conditional on minutes.
  • none quality means unknown, not bad.
  • Scenario Gain overstates real gain twice over — once when the outgoing player is benched, again when the incoming player only starts by displacing an artefact. Trust rows where the outgoing player is genuinely in the XI, or where the outgoing player is at 0.0 xP (then Gain, not Raw, is the honest number).
  • The scenario table only validates transfers one at a time. Re-run combined arithmetic from sell prices and give the funding order.
  • Club-cap blockers expire. Re-check every standing rejection after any sale.
  • Dataset club labels drift from reality. Follow the table for cap arithmetic and say so when it looks odd.
  • The model's own best XI ignores injury flags (GW10: it started Calvert-Lewin at 75%, all three passes).
  • The model has no view on the vice-captain. Vice must not share the captain's fixture — check the opponent, not just the club.
  • The briefing does not always include the saved lineup. GW7 pass 2 through GW10 pass 3 had no slots 1-11 block. When that happens, give the full XI and bench order from scratch and ask the manager to verify.
  • Briefing lead time varies wildly — 47 hours to 10 minutes. When it is short, write action-first: one transfer, the XI, the armband, nothing else.
  • Price/net-transfer flags are volatile between passes. A large one-sided outflow on an owned player (Mbeumo in GW3, GW10 p2 and GW10 p3) is the only crowd signal available when search fails — flag it as a presser check, never act on it alone.
  • Advice execution is inconsistent. Executed: GW6, GW7 p1, GW8 p2, GW9 p2. Not executed: GW8 p3, GW9 p1, GW10 p1, GW10 p2. Two consecutive non-executions within one gameweek is new — lead with the single most important action, say plainly that it is a repeat, and put the deadline distance up front.
  • Model bias: GW1 -2.38 (MAE 4.88), GW2 -2.83 (3.18), GW3 -1.06 (2.52), GW4 -0.10 (1.98), GW5 -1.46 (3.35), GW6 -0.33 (2.38), GW7 -0.34 (2.39), GW8 +1.44 (2.51), GW9 -0.20 (2.89). Sign unstable; trust the MAE (~2.5-2.9). Haaland alone has missed by 5+ three times, most recently GW9 (6.9 predicted, 2 actual).
  • Chip discipline. 3xc declined eleven times; pencilled GW11 (Haaland v FUL H, 8.6), backstop ~GW15, first-half chips expire GW19. BB has projected 4-13 all season against a 12 bar, never with a double. Wildcard re-tested GW9 and GW10 (all three passes): HOLD. Trigger — if the João Pedro slot stays dead into GW11 alongside Ødegaard/Mitchell, that is two genuine holes and the wildcard case opens by GW12.
  • Web search unreliable in this harness (GW1-GW10, all empty). Say so plainly; make minutes calls conditional on the manager checking the presser; never substitute recall.
