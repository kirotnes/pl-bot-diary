What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors — and when the defence expires. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when fit. White was the canonical case (~1.0/GW projected against 11, 7, 2 on three 90s); a White sale topped the board five weeks running and was correctly declined every time until an FPL d flag made it a minutes call. Ødegaard was the long-running hold, closed in GW8 — and he then scored 11 in GW10 and started again in GW11. Closure means "no longer trusted to beat his projection", not "never fielded".
  • The reverse artefact: a player the model still rates who has quietly lost his place. Thiaw sat in the model's own best XI in GW9 at 12.7 xP5 having played 1 minute in GW8. Maguire repeated it in GW11 — 45 minutes in GW10 and 0/0/0 in three, still carrying a live projection. Scan the form table for a sudden minutes collapse every week; it is the cheapest edge available and the one signal the xP model is structurally blind to.
  • Availability flags move between passes within the same gameweek. N.Williams: d 75% (arm) in GW9 p1, 100% hours later in p2, d 75% (ankle) by GW11. Re-read the availability block every run; never carry a flag forward from a previous note.
  • Re-check every standing rejection when its premise changes. Tavernier was rejected three times on Ødegaard's artefact and scored 10 the week it was executed. De Cuyper was rejected in GW8 and GW10 partly on elite ownership running below xEO — by GW11 that had flipped and the row topped the board; it was executed. The elite-vs-xEO reading is not stable across weeks; re-read it, never quote the last note's number.
  • Read the elite-vs-xEO gap in both directions. Elite above xEO means informed money is already there; elite below means the crowd is on someone the elite avoid (Evanilson, GW11: elite ~10% v xEO ~26%). Direction only; never the sole reason for a move.
  • An un-executed row can grow between passes — and can also hold. JP → Barry was +6.3, then +8.5, then +8.5. Re-price every repeat.
  • Absence of a form line is not absence of minutes. The form block only covers owned players, so an unowned candidate has no form row by construction. Say minutes are unverified; read ownership %, data quality and the rise flag as weak inference that he starts — never as evidence. (Barry: three passes unverified, then 16 points. Vuskovic in GW11: same situation.)
  • A dead squad slot is not automatically a hole. The test is "does the starting eleven break". But a slot that is both dead and flagged is the cleanest use of a marginal FT — GW11 p2 took N.Williams (75%, 11.7 xP5) over equal-value alternatives on exactly that basis.
  • Prefer the transfer whose outgoing player has NO artefact, and when gains tie, the tiebreak is what stays behind and whether the move fixes a flag or a cap.
  • Club-cap blockers expire — and caps can sit for a very long time. MUN sat on three from GW1 to GW11, named as a cost nine times before a move finally cleared it as a side effect. GW11 p2 deliberately created a new one at BHA (De Cuyper, Groß, Vuskovic) for +3.7. Named costs do not fix themselves — put a review date on this one.
  • Scenario Gain overstates real gain twice over — once when the outgoing player is benched, again when the incoming player only starts by displacing an artefact. Trust rows where the outgoing player is genuinely in the XI, or at 0.0 xP.
  • The scenario table only validates transfers one at a time. Re-run combined arithmetic from sell prices and give the funding order.
  • Bank headroom is part of the price.
  • GK override, resolved. Start whichever of Lammens/Kinsky has the better fixture; on equal FDR take the home side. Stop re-litigating.
  • Defensive-contribution players are systematically under-projected, but that floor is conditional on minutes.
  • none quality means unknown, not bad.
  • Dataset club labels drift from reality. Follow the table for cap arithmetic and say so when it looks odd.
  • The model's own best XI ignores injury flags.
  • The model has no view on the vice-captain. Vice must not share the captain's fixture — check the opponent, not just the club.
  • The briefing does not always include the saved lineup. GW7 p2 through GW11 p2 (twelve consecutive passes) had no slots 1-11 block. When that happens, give the full XI and bench order from scratch and ask the manager to verify.
  • Briefing lead time varies wildly — 39.7 hours to 10 minutes. When it is short, write action-first: one transfer, the XI, the armband, nothing else.
  • Price/net-transfer flags are volatile between passes. A large one-sided outflow on an owned player (Mbeumo in GW3, GW10 p2, GW10 p3, GW11 p1, GW11 p2 — five times) is the only crowd signal available when search fails. Flag it as a presser check, never act on it alone.
  • Advice execution is inconsistent. Executed: GW6, GW7 p1, GW8 p2, GW9 p2, GW10 p3, GW11 p1. Not executed: GW8 p3, GW9 p1, GW10 p1, GW10 p2. Lead with the single most important action, say plainly when it is a repeat, and put the deadline distance up front.
  • Model bias: GW1 -2.38 (MAE 4.88), GW2 -2.83 (3.18), GW3 -1.06 (2.52), GW4 -0.10 (1.98), GW5 -1.46 (3.35), GW6 -0.33 (2.38), GW7 -0.34 (2.39), GW8 +1.44 (2.51), GW9 -0.20 (2.89), GW10 +0.12 (4.18). Sign unstable; trust the MAE (~2.5-3.0, spiking to 4.2). A +3.7 five-gameweek edge is inside that noise. Haaland has missed by 5+ four times, most recently GW10 (7.8 predicted, 2 actual) and GW9 (6.9 / 2). Price his projection down before any chip decision.
  • Chip discipline. 3xc declined thirteen times. Hard rule: re-test GW16, commit no later than GW18; first-half chips expire GW19. BB has projected 4-13 all season against a 12 bar — GW11 p2 was the closest (~13) and still declined on FDR-4 bench fixtures and no double. FH never had a target. Wildcard re-tested GW9, GW10 (x3), GW11 (x2): HOLD. Next re-test GW13. New: four unused chips against nine remaining gameweeks — from GW13 the test becomes "better than losing the chip", not "the best possible week".
  • Web search unreliable in this harness (GW1-GW11, all empty). Say so plainly; make minutes calls conditional on the manager checking the presser; never substitute recall.
