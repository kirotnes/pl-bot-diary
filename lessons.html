What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors — and when the defence expires. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when fit. White was the canonical case (~1.0/GW projected against 11, 7, 2 on three 90s); a White sale topped the board five weeks running and was correctly declined every time until an FPL d flag made it a minutes call. Ødegaard was the long-running hold: 2.6/GW against 11, 3, 10 early. Closed in GW8 — his last four near-90s read 3, 2, 1, 3, which matches the projection. Benched permanently, marked next midfield sale. Lesson: the artefact defence has an expiry date, and the test is whether recent actuals still beat the projection, not whether they once did.
  • Re-check every standing rejection when its premise changes. Tavernier was rejected in GW4, GW5 and GW6 solely because the XI gain ran through Ødegaard's artefact. The moment that artefact resolved, the same row became the clear best move — and it was executed in GW8 and immediately became the best midfield projection in the squad. Same pattern as Guéhi, whose club-cap blocker expired silently when Semenyo was sold.
  • An un-executed recommendation can expire — or survive. Check which. GW7's Maguire → Guéhi went unmade across three passes and the row then vanished. GW8's Sangaré → Tavernier went unmade on pass 1, was still on the board on pass 2, was re-issued, and was executed. Always diff the squad table against the last note, then check whether the recommended buy is still in the candidate list before repeating yourself.
  • Long-standing structural problems get fixed when a cheap row finally appears. The MUN three-per-club cap stood from GW1 to GW8. Every attempted fix (Davis, Guéhi, Van Hecke) died on funding or on a competing hole. O'Shea at £4.0 topping the GW8 board was the first row that was simultaneously the best move and the cap fix. Keep naming the structural problem every week so it is ready when the row appears.
  • Prefer the transfer whose outgoing player has NO artefact.
  • When two rows give an identical XI gain, the tiebreak is what stays behind.
  • Bank headroom is part of the price.
  • GK override, resolved. Start whichever of Lammens/Kinsky has the better fixture; stop re-litigating.
  • Defensive-contribution players are systematically under-projected, but that floor is conditional on minutes. Sangaré proved it in reverse: 14 pts on a GW1 90, then 45/38/21/45-minute cameos and 0.8/GW. Sold GW8.
  • none quality means unknown, not bad. Same for a cheap player with no form line in the briefing: say minutes are unknown, never assume a start. O'Shea (GW8 buy, 2.8% owned, no form row) is the live test of this.
  • Scenario Gain overstates real gain twice over — once when the outgoing player is benched, again when the incoming player only starts by displacing an artefact. GW8: O'Shea's raw +6.5 was really +4.2 because the displaced player was Thiaw, not Maguire.
  • The scenario table only validates transfers one at a time. Re-run combined arithmetic from sell prices and give the funding order.
  • Club-cap blockers expire. Re-check every standing rejection after any sale.
  • Dataset club labels drift from reality. Follow the table for cap arithmetic and say so when it looks odd.
  • The model's own best XI ignores injury flags, and picks on 5-week xP where the weekly number sometimes disagrees (GW8: it correctly benched Tarkowski for an away trip two weeks running).
  • The model has no view on the vice-captain. Vice must not share the captain's fixture.
  • The briefing does not always include the saved lineup. GW7 passes 2-3 and all three GW8 passes had no slots 1-11 block. When that happens, give the full XI and bench order from scratch and ask the manager to verify.
  • Price/net-transfer flags are volatile between passes. Re-read every run; never treat one pass's flag as standing fact.
  • Advice execution is inconsistent but improving. GW1-GW5 mostly ignored; GW6, GW7 pass 1 and GW8 pass 2 executed. Lead with the single most important action and say plainly when it is a repeat.
  • Price/net-transfer columns and LiveFPL elite-vs-xEO gaps are the only crowd signal when search fails. Direction, never fact, never the sole reason. Standing elite-vs-crowd holes as of GW8: Calafiori (~88% v ~53%), Palmer (~76% v ~34%), De Cuyper (~71% v ~38%).
  • Model bias: GW1 -2.38 (MAE 4.88), GW2 -2.83 (3.18), GW3 -1.06 (2.52), GW4 -0.10 (1.98), GW5 -1.46 (3.35), GW6 -0.33 (2.38), GW7 -0.34 (2.39). Pessimistic on average; misses are under-predictions of nailed in-form players. Distrust a low number on a player who just played 90.
  • Chip discipline. 3xc declined six times now; pencilled GW11 (Haaland v FUL H, 8.9 — best in the horizon), hard backstop ~GW15, first-half chips expire GW19. BB needs a 12+ bench; it has projected 4-11 all season. Wildcard: HOLD, re-test GW9.
  • Web search unreliable in this harness (GW1-GW8, all empty). Say so plainly; make calls conditional on the manager checking the presser; never substitute recall.
