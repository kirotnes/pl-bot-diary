What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when fit. White was the canonical case: ~1.0/GW projected while scoring 11, 7, 2 on three 90s; a White sale topped the board for five straight gameweeks and was correctly declined every time — until GW5, when FPL flagged him d 75% and the sale went through on minutes, not on xP. The artefact is never the reason to sell; an availability flag is. Ødegaard is the live case (2.1/GW projected; 11, 3, 10, 3 actual).
  • The model is badly wrong on Kinsky specifically. Predicted 0.9 → actual 6 (GW3); predicted 1.1 → actual 7 (GW4); 16 pts in four 90s against a 1.3/GW projection, while Lammens (2.7/GW projected) has returned 1, 2, 2, 2. Kept Kinsky over Lammens in both the GW5 third and fourth passes, on that evidence plus elite ownership (32.4% v 0.9%). Track whether this holds — if Kinsky reverts, the override was wrong.
  • Defensive-contribution players are systematically under-projected, but that floor is conditional on minutes. M.Sangaré: 14, 4, 0 (45 mins), 4 against 0.8/GW. Bench player whose upgrade does not change the XI.
  • none quality means unknown, not bad.
  • Scenario Gain overstates real gain twice over — once when the outgoing player is benched, again when the incoming player only starts by displacing an artefact. Sangaré/Ødegaard → Tavernier has shown +12 to +13 for seven consecutive gameweeks and been declined seven times, always because Tavernier enters only over Ødegaard.
  • The best use of a marginal FT is a hole, not an upgrade. GW5: Georginio → Calvert-Lewin was taken not on xP (+6 board, ~+4 honest) but because a 75%-flagged starting forward had zero forward cover on the bench and Georginio had 0 minutes in three gameweeks. Frame these as insurance and say the XI gain is zero.
  • The model's own best XI ignores injury flags (started Georginio at 75% doubt with zero minutes). Always re-check against the saved XI and the availability block.
  • The model has no view on the vice-captain. The saved vice was João Pedro at d 75% in both the GW5 third and fourth passes — the fix was recommended, not executed, and had to be re-issued. Check the vice against the flags every single week; it is the cheapest fix available.
  • The saved lineup drifts between passes and advice is frequently not executed at all. GW5 ran to four passes; the third and fourth both found the same 3-5-2 with Lammens and Mitchell benched, i.e. nothing from the third pass was applied. Never assume last pass's XI is saved — re-read slots 1-11 from the snapshot and state the delta out loud, every time. Lead the report with the free changes, especially when the briefing arrives close to the deadline (GW5 fourth pass: 41 minutes).
  • Verify which club a sale actually frees, and which player actually left. GW5 first pass claimed a White sale dropped MUN off the cap (White is ARS). The GW4 note called the executed move "Maguire → Tarkowski" when in fact White left and Maguire stayed. Count clubs and confirm departures from the squad table, never from the previous narrative.
  • Club-cap discipline beats a 3-point xP edge. Guéhi is listed at MCI here; buying him caps MCI at three with Haaland and Semenyo and stacks correlation onto the captain. Declined GW3, GW4, GW5.
  • Fixture correlation is invisible to the model. GW4 had four XI players in MUN v MCI; GW5 has two (Haaland, Semenyo) with the captain among them. Name it every week, including correlations a transfer creates (CLW v Mitchell in GW5).
  • Dataset club labels drift from reality (Van Hecke listed TOT, De Cuyper listed BHA, Semenyo and Guéhi listed MCI). Follow the table for cap arithmetic.
  • Price/net-transfer columns are the only crowd signal when search fails. Do not buy into a heavy outflow; flag a big outflow on a holding as a presser check.
  • Model bias: GW1 -2.38 (MAE 4.88), GW2 -2.83 (3.18), GW3 -1.06 (2.52), GW4 -0.10 (1.98). Pessimism has converged. Usable prior from GW5 on, but still distrust a low number on a player who just played 90.
  • Dead bench capital is a structural drag the xP model cannot express. GW5: ~£15.9m in non-starters plus no premium midfielder, rank at 5.27m. Single FTs cannot unwind that inside the chip window — a wildcard case argued from squad structure, not a scenario row. WC decision deadline GW6; first-half chips expire GW19.
  • Banking an FT beats a +3 marginal upgrade when a wildcard decision is one week away. GW5: declined Maguire → Van Hecke on exactly this ground.
  • Web search unreliable in this harness (GW1-GW5, all empty). Say so plainly; make calls conditional on the manager checking the presser; never substitute recall.
