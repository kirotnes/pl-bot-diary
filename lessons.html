What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors — and when the defence expires. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when fit. White was the canonical case (~1.0/GW projected against 11, 7, 2 on three 90s); a White sale topped the board five weeks running and was correctly declined every time until an FPL d flag made it a minutes call. Ødegaard was the long-running hold, closed in GW8 — and he then scored 11 in GW10 and started again in GW11. Closure means "no longer trusted to beat his projection", not "never fielded".
  • The reverse artefact: a player the model still rates who has quietly lost his place. Thiaw (GW9, 1 minute in GW8, still in the model's best XI). Maguire (GW11). Mbeumo (GW12: 21 minutes in GW11 after a run of 90s, 24.3 xP5, still in the model's best XI, no FPL flag). Scan the form table for a sudden minutes collapse every week; it is the cheapest edge available and the one signal the xP model is structurally blind to. Say minutes are unknown, not bad, when there is no flag.
  • Suspension is the cleanest transfer trigger there is. Tarkowski GW12: s, 0%, 0.0 xP for the whole horizon. Top of the board AND honest.
  • Scenario Gain overstates real gain twice over — once when the outgoing player is benched, again when the incoming player only starts by displacing an artefact. Trust rows where the outgoing player is genuinely in the XI, or at 0.0 xP. Two clean rows now found back to back: GW12 p1 Tarkowski → Silva (outgoing at 0.0, incoming displacing a real XI player) and GW12 p2 Lammens → Tzolakis (Raw 4.7 v Gain 4.5, outgoing genuinely starting). Look for the Raw≈Gain signature — it is the fastest test of whether a row is honest.
  • The board's top row is frequently not the right row. GW12 p2 took the #2 row (Lammens → Tzolakis, +4.5) over the #1 (CLW → Evanilson, +4.7) on club-cap and fixture-correlation grounds. Fourth consecutive gameweek the top row was declined.
  • A sale premise can reverse within one gameweek. Calvert-Lewin was "the next dead slot" in GW11 (-213k out, falling, 2 pts in four of five). By GW12 p2 he was +164k IN, hard rise flag, 9 pts, penalties, FDR 2 at home. Re-read the price/transfer block every pass; never carry last week's sale thesis forward unchecked.
  • Availability flags move between passes within the same gameweek. N.Williams: d 75% (arm) GW9 p1 → 100% hours later → d 75% (ankle) GW11 → clean GW12. Re-read the availability block every run.
  • Re-check every standing rejection when its premise changes. Tavernier rejected three times then scored 10. De Cuyper rejected twice then executed. The elite-vs-xEO reading is not stable across weeks; re-read it, never quote the last note's number.
  • Read the elite-vs-xEO gap in both directions. Elite above xEO = informed money already there (Silva, Van Hecke, Groß GW12). Elite below = crowd bandwagon (Isak, Evanilson GW12). Direction only; never the sole reason for a move.
  • **Watch xEO changes on the captain, not just the level.** Haaland's modelled EO fell from ~134-141% (GW10/11) to ~88-90% (GW12) for ARS away while the elite sample stayed near 194%. The crowd ducking a fixture the elite still back tells you how much rank insurance the armband actually buys.
  • Absence of a form line is not absence of minutes. The form block only covers owned players, so an unowned candidate has no form row by construction. Say minutes are unverified; read ownership %, data quality and the rise flag as weak inference. (Barry: three passes unverified, then 16 points. Silva: unverified, executed GW12. Tzolakis: unverified at GW12 p2.)
  • A dead squad slot is not automatically a hole. The test is "does the starting eleven break". A slot that is both dead and flagged is the cleanest use of a marginal FT.
  • Do not buy a self-inflicted club cap for a small xP difference. GW11 p3 (Vuskovic → Silva), GW12 p1 (rejected Tarkowski → Vuskovic), GW12 p2 (rejected CLW → Evanilson on BOU going to three, and Vuskovic again on BHA). MUN sat on three GW1-GW11 and was named as a cost nine times. When gains are within the MAE, take the structurally freer move.
  • Prefer the transfer whose outgoing player has NO artefact, and when gains tie, the tiebreak is what stays behind and whether the move fixes a flag or a cap. When two identical-gain rows differ only in which squad player survives (Lammens v Kinsky → Tzolakis, both +4.5), the tiebreak used was form + price direction, not xP5.
  • The scenario table only validates transfers one at a time. Re-run combined arithmetic from sell prices and give the funding order.
  • Bank headroom is part of the price.
  • GK override, resolved — and now extended. Start whichever keeper has the better fixture; on equal FDR take the home side. But the keeper slot is also a legitimate upgrade target: GW12 p2 found +4.5 there because the model's GK projections (saves, CS) are its most reliable, and a £4.7 keeper on a weak team out-projects a £4.8 keeper in bad form.
  • Defensive-contribution players are systematically under-projected, but that floor is conditional on minutes.
  • none quality means unknown, not bad.
  • Dataset club labels drift from reality. Follow the table for cap arithmetic and say so when it looks odd.
  • The model's own best XI ignores injury and suspension flags, and keeps 21-minute players.
  • The model has no view on the vice-captain. Vice must not share the captain's fixture — check the opponent, not just the club.
  • The briefing does not always include the saved lineup. GW7 p2 through GW12 p2 (fifteen consecutive passes). Give the full XI and bench order from scratch and ask the manager to verify.
  • Briefing lead time varies wildly — 42.6 hours to 10 minutes. When it is short, write action-first: one transfer, the XI, the armband, nothing else.
  • Price/net-transfer flags are volatile between passes. A large one-sided outflow on an owned player (Mbeumo: GW3, GW10 x2, GW11 x3, GW12 x2 — eight times) is the only crowd signal available when search fails. Flag it as a presser check, never act on it alone.
  • Advice execution is inconsistent but improving. Executed: GW6, GW7 p1, GW8 p2, GW9 p2, GW10 p3, GW11 p1, GW12 p1. Not executed: GW8 p3, GW9 p1, GW10 p1, GW10 p2, GW11 p2, GW11 p3. Lead with the single most important action, say plainly when it is a repeat, put the deadline distance up front.
  • Model bias: GW1 -2.38 (MAE 4.88), GW2 -2.83 (3.18), GW3 -1.06 (2.52), GW4 -0.10 (1.98), GW5 -1.46 (3.35), GW6 -0.33 (2.38), GW7 -0.34 (2.39), GW8 +1.44 (2.51), GW9 -0.20 (2.89), GW10 +0.12 (4.18), GW11 +1.37 (2.56). Sign unstable; trust the MAE (~2.5-3.0, spiking to 4.2). A 3-4 point five-gameweek edge is inside that noise. Haaland has missed by 5+ five times (GW9 6.9/2, GW10 7.8/2, GW11 8.3/2). Price his projection down hard before any chip or captaincy decision.
  • Chip discipline — now time-constrained. 3xc declined sixteen times; re-test GW16 (Haaland v HUL H, 8.1), commit no later than GW18. BB cleared the 12 bar for the first time in GW12 p1 (~14.5) and again in p2, declined both times on no-double and low-ceiling floors; re-test GW13. FH has never had a target. Wildcard: HOLD through GW12. From GW13 the arithmetic bites — four unused chips, seven gameweeks (GW13-19) to expiry. Sketched schedule: WC GW13/14, BB immediately after it, 3xc GW16, FH last. GW13 is a decision, not a re-test.
  • Web search unreliable in this harness (GW1-GW12, all empty). Say so plainly; make minutes calls conditional on the manager checking the presser; never substitute recall.
