What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when fit. White was the canonical case: ~1.0/GW projected while scoring 11, 7, 2 on three 90s; a White sale topped the board for five straight gameweeks and was correctly declined every time — until GW5, when FPL flagged him d 75% and the sale went through on minutes, not on xP. The artefact is never the reason to sell; an availability flag is. Applied again in GW6 (Semenyo) and GW7 (Szoboszlai, executed). Ødegaard is the long-running hold case — 2.6/GW projected against 11, 3, 10, 3, 2, 1 actual. Held a ninth week through GW7 pass 3; stated trigger is a blank at Forest, after which he is the sale ahead of Sangaré.
  • Prefer the transfer whose outgoing player has NO artefact. GW7: Ødegaard → Semenyo topped the board at +7.6 and was declined three passes running for Maguire → Guéhi at +7.5, because Maguire's 2.3/GW matches his actual 4-2-1-2-2 while Ødegaard's does not. When two rows score the same, take the one where the model is demonstrably right about the man leaving.
  • Bank headroom is part of the price. The Semenyo row left £0.0 and would have blocked the following defender move.
  • GK override, resolved. The GW6 reversal to Lammens cost exactly 1 point. Start whichever has the better fixture; stop re-litigating.
  • Defensive-contribution players are systematically under-projected, but that floor is conditional on minutes. Sangaré is now dead weight (0.7/GW, 38 mins in GW6).
  • none quality means unknown, not bad.
  • Scenario Gain overstates real gain twice over — once when the outgoing player is benched, again when the incoming player only starts by displacing an artefact.
  • The scenario table only validates transfers one at a time. Re-run combined arithmetic from sell prices and give the funding order.
  • Club-cap blockers expire. Guéhi was declined GW3-GW7(pass 1) partly on the MCI cap; selling Semenyo in GW6 silently unblocked him. Re-check every standing rejection after any sale.
  • Dataset club labels drift from reality (Guéhi and Semenyo listed MCI, Rogers listed CHE). Follow the table for cap arithmetic, and say so when it looks odd.
  • The model's own best XI ignores injury flags. Always re-check against the saved XI and the availability block.
  • The model has no view on the vice-captain. Check against flags weekly; vice must not share the captain's fixture.
  • The briefing does not always include the saved lineup. GW7 passes 2 and 3 had no slots 1-11 block at all. When that happens, give the full XI and bench order from scratch and ask the manager to verify.
  • Price/net-transfer flags are volatile between passes. Mbeumo read -98%/-112% (likelihood -5) in GW7 pass 2 and -3.4% (likelihood -1) in pass 3, same gameweek. Do not treat a single pass's flag as a standing fact; re-read it every run.
  • Advice execution is inconsistent. GW1-GW5 mostly ignored; GW6 and GW7 pass 1 executed in full; GW7 pass 2 (Maguire → Guéhi) NOT executed and had to be re-issued with 2.7h left. Always diff the squad table against the last note before writing.
  • Price/net-transfer columns and LiveFPL elite-vs-xEO gaps are the only crowd signal when search fails. Use for direction, never quote as fact, never as the sole reason for a move.
  • Model bias: GW1 -2.38 (MAE 4.88), GW2 -2.83 (3.18), GW3 -1.06 (2.52), GW4 -0.10 (1.98), GW5 -1.46 (3.35), GW6 -0.33 (2.38). Pessimistic on average; misses are under-predictions of nailed in-form players. Distrust a low number on a player who just played 90.
  • Chip discipline. 3xc declined three times for GW7 (Haaland 8.2 v IPS H): +8 is the floor of what a chip should earn and the same 8.2 recurs at GW11 (FUL H). Rule — take 3xc by ~GW15 on the best remaining Haaland home fixture if no double or 9+ projection appears. First-half chips expire GW19. Wildcard: HOLD, re-test GW9. BB needs a 12+ bench; it has projected 4-10 all season.
  • Web search unreliable in this harness (GW1-GW7, all empty). Say so plainly; make calls conditional on the manager checking the presser; never substitute recall.
