What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when they are good and fit. White is the canonical case: ~1.1/GW projected while scoring 11, 7 and 2 on three full 90s. A White sale has topped the scenario board six gameweeks running and has been correctly declined every time. Ødegaard is the same case (2.0/GW projected; 11, 3, 10 actual). Do not sell on these numbers; check whether the player is nailed now.
  • Defensive-contribution players are systematically under-projected. M.Sangaré: 14 then 4 on two 90s against a 0.7/GW projection. But as of GW3 he played only 45 minutes, so that floor is now conditional on minutes and he has become the next identified sale rather than a reliable bench asset.
  • none quality means unknown, not bad. Refuse confident calls until current-season minutes accumulate.
  • Scenario-table gains overstate real gain when the outgoing player was a bench player. Read Gain, not Raw — then discount again if the outgoing player was benched in the saved XI even though the model's own best XI started him. GW4 second pass: Maguire → Tarkowski showed +11.3, honest value +4 to +6, because the real swap was Tarkowski-for-White.
  • The model's own best XI ignores injury flags. GW3 and GW4 it started Georginio at 75% doubt with zero recent minutes. Always re-check flags against both the saved XI and the model's suggestion.
  • Check the bench before checking transfers — every single week. The lineup fix beat the best transfer in GW1, GW2, GW3 and the GW4 first pass. In the GW4 second pass it did not — the saved XI was already correct after the Collins→Mitchell fix. So: check first, but do not assume there is always something broken.
  • The saved lineup drifts and advice is only ever partly executed. Always re-read slots 1-11 and state the delta explicitly. Note the briefing's "Starting XI fixtures" block is the saved XI; the "best XI by the model" block is not.
  • Club-cap discipline beats a 0.7-point xP edge. GW4: took Tarkowski over Guéhi because Guéhi would have capped MCI at three alongside Haaland and Semenyo. Concentration risk plus a blocked future transfer is worth more than the projection difference.
  • Vice-captain fixture diversification. Semenyo is listed at MCI, same as Haaland, so he is never eligible as vice. Mbeumo (MUN) is also ineligible in any GW where MUN play MCI — that was GW4. Szoboszlai is the default fallback.
  • Elite-vs-xEO gaps are direction only. Both Guéhi (elite 0.1% v xEO 18.3%) and Tarkowski (0.8% v 11.2%) show the crowd going somewhere elite managers are not. Name it, price it, never make it the sole reason.
  • Price-change/net-transfer columns are the only crowd signal when search fails. Do not buy into a heavy outflow; flag a big outflow on a player you own as a presser check.
  • Model bias: GW1 -2.38 (MAE 4.88), GW2 -2.83 (MAE 3.18), GW3 -1.06 (MAE 2.52). Consistently pessimistic, misses are under-predictions of nailed, in-form players. Bias shrinking as current-season data accumulates; still treat low xP on a player who just played 90 minutes with active suspicion.
  • Web search is unreliable in this harness (GW1-GW4 all empty, five passes). When there is no news layer, say so plainly and make recommendations conditional on the manager checking the presser — never substitute recall.
