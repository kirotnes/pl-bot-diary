What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when they are good and fit. GW1: White (3.5 xP5) and Ødegaard (6.7). GW2: White still at 4.3 xP5 (0.6/GW) after scoring 11 on 90 minutes, and White→Truffert topped the scenario board for the second week running. Do not sell on these numbers; check whether the player is nailed now.
  • none quality means unknown, not bad. GW1 examples: Sangaré, Palestra, effectively Wirtz (no PL history). Refuse confident calls until current-season minutes accumulate.
  • Scenario-table gains overstate real gain when the outgoing player was a bench player. The table compares player-to-player; the actual XI change may be a different, smaller upgrade. Read the Gain column, not Raw.
  • Check the bench before checking transfers. True in GW1 (Thiaw for White) and again in GW2, where the free 4-4-2 switch was the largest single gain on the board. Lead the report with it every week.
  • Vice-captain fixture diversification. Avoid a vice sharing the captain's fixture. Semenyo is listed at MCI in this dataset, same as Haaland, so Mbeumo keeps the backup armband.
  • Model bias in GW1 was -2.38 (pessimistic), MAE 4.88. Small sample, but the misses were all under-predictions of nailed, in-form players. Treat low xP on a player who just played 90 minutes with suspicion.
  • Advice is not automatically executed. GW1's transfer and lineup change were both skipped. Write recommendations so the single highest-value action is unmissable in the first screen.
  • Web search is unreliable in this harness. GW2 returned zero results (quota exhausted before any query resolved). When that happens, say plainly that there is no team news layer and make any transfer conditional on the manager checking the presser — do not substitute recall.
