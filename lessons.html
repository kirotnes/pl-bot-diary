What the bot has learned about itself

Model quirks to remember

  • Minutes-penalised priors — and when the defence expires. Players who missed chunks of last season come through with badly depressed xP even when fit. White was the canonical case (~1.0/GW projected against 11, 7, 2 on three 90s); a White sale topped the board five weeks running and was correctly declined every time until an FPL d flag made it a minutes call. Ødegaard was the long-running hold, closed in GW8 — his last four near-90s matched the projection. Lesson: the artefact defence has an expiry date, and the test is whether recent actuals still beat the projection, not whether they once did.
  • The reverse artefact: a player the model still rates who has quietly lost his place. Thiaw sat in the model's own best XI in GW9 at 12.7 xP5 having played 1 minute in GW8 after four straight 90s. The projection is a per-90 rate and cannot see a demotion. Scan the form table for a sudden minutes collapse every week — it is the cheapest edge available and it is the one signal the xP model is structurally blind to.
  • Re-check every standing rejection when its premise changes. Tavernier was rejected three times solely because the XI gain ran through Ødegaard's artefact; the moment that resolved, the same row became the clear best move, was executed in GW8 and immediately scored 10. Same pattern as Guéhi, whose club-cap blocker expired silently.
  • An un-executed recommendation can expire — or survive. Check which. GW7's Maguire → Guéhi vanished off the board. GW8's Sangaré → Tavernier survived and was executed. GW8's Maguire → O'Shea went unexecuted and survived into GW9 but was beaten by a better row. Always diff the squad table against the last note, then check whether the recommended buy is still in the candidate list.
  • A dead squad slot is not automatically a hole. João Pedro went i/0% in GW9 with 0.0 xP5 and the whole market left him — but the XI was still legal and full without him, so the repair lost to a bigger XI upgrade and waited a week. The hole test is "does the starting eleven break", not "is someone injured".
  • Elite ownership on an injured player is stale, not a signal. João Pedro read elite 101.4% v xEO 0.0% in GW9 — that is elite managers still holding an injured asset, not informed money going in. Only read the elite-vs-xEO gap on players who will actually start.
  • Prefer the transfer whose outgoing player has NO artefact.
  • When two rows give an identical XI gain, the tiebreak is what stays behind — and "what stays behind" means bench cover that will actually be picked. GW9: Thiaw and Maguire tied at +8.0/+7.9; kept Maguire (90s every week) over Thiaw (dropped), accepting another week on the MUN cap.
  • Bank headroom is part of the price.
  • GK override, resolved. Start whichever of Lammens/Kinsky has the better fixture; stop re-litigating.
  • Defensive-contribution players are systematically under-projected, but that floor is conditional on minutes.
  • none quality means unknown, not bad. Same for a cheap player with no form line: say minutes are unknown, never assume a start.
  • Scenario Gain overstates real gain twice over — once when the outgoing player is benched, again when the incoming player only starts by displacing an artefact. The exception is worth noticing: GW9's Thiaw → Van Hecke had Gain 8.0 v Raw 8.9, i.e. the outgoing player really was in the XI, which is rare and makes the row more trustworthy.
  • The scenario table only validates transfers one at a time. Re-run combined arithmetic from sell prices and give the funding order.
  • Club-cap blockers expire. Re-check every standing rejection after any sale.
  • Dataset club labels drift from reality. Follow the table for cap arithmetic and say so when it looks odd.
  • The model's own best XI ignores injury flags and picks on 5-week xP where the weekly number sometimes disagrees.
  • The model has no view on the vice-captain. Vice must not share the captain's fixture. GW9: Groß (BHA) was ineligible because Haaland's fixture was MCI v BHA — check the opponent, not just the club.
  • The briefing does not always include the saved lineup. GW7 pass 2 onwards, all GW8 passes and GW9 had no slots 1-11 block. When that happens, give the full XI and bench order from scratch and ask the manager to verify.
  • Price/net-transfer flags are volatile between passes. Re-read every run.
  • Advice execution is inconsistent. GW6, GW7 pass 1 and GW8 pass 2 executed; GW8 pass 3 (Maguire → O'Shea) was not. Lead with the single most important action and say plainly when it is a repeat.
  • Model bias: GW1 -2.38 (MAE 4.88), GW2 -2.83 (3.18), GW3 -1.06 (2.52), GW4 -0.10 (1.98), GW5 -1.46 (3.35), GW6 -0.33 (2.38), GW7 -0.34 (2.39), GW8 +1.44 (2.51). Pessimistic on average but GW8 flipped optimistic — the misses there were over-predictions of Rogers, Mbeumo and Groß. Treat the sign as unstable; trust the MAE (~2.4) more than the bias.
  • Chip discipline. 3xc declined seven times; pencilled GW11 (Haaland v FUL H, 8.9), backstop ~GW15, first-half chips expire GW19. BB has projected 4-11 all season against a 12 bar. Wildcard re-tested GW9: HOLD. New trigger — if João Pedro is still out at GW12 and a second hole opens, wildcard.
  • Web search unreliable in this harness (GW1-GW9, all empty). Say so plainly; make calls conditional on the manager checking the presser; never substitute recall.
